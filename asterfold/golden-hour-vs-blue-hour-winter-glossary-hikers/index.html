    The glossary

    Golden hour
    The stretch when the Sun is low: by the convention most planning apps use, from about 6° above the horizon to 4° below it. Warm, long shadows, soft contrast. In December at mid-northern latitudes it is short and early; the calculator above gives yours.Convention; the exact angles vary by source.
    Blue hour
    After sunset (and before sunrise), roughly while the Sun is 4° to 6° below the horizon: the sky goes deep blue, city lights balance it. Golden hour vs blue hour is a question of which side of the horizon the Sun is on.Convention, as above.
    Civil twilight, or headlamp time
    Ends when the Sun is 6° below the horizon. Until then you can usually still see the trail without a light; after it, you cannot. For hikers this is the number that matters more than sunset.USNO definition.
    Nautical and astronomical twilight
    Sun 12° and 18° below. Nautical: horizon still visible at sea. Astronomical: the sky is fully dark for stars. Only the second one matters for the Milky Way; neither matters for walking down.USNO definitions.
    Sunset bearing, or azimuth
    The compass direction the Sun sets in, measured from north. It swings through the year: furthest south around 21 December, furthest north around 21 June. A summit that faces 250° gets a clean December sunset and a blocked June one.Geometry; Asterfold draws the year's range on its sky dome.
    Solar altitude
    How high the Sun is above the horizon in degrees. All the words above are just altitudes with names.
    Alpenglow
    The pink-red light on peaks after the Sun has set at your spot but not yet at the summit's height. Lasts minutes. Needs clear air to the west.
    Belt of Venus and Earth's shadow
    Opposite the sunset: a pink band above a blue-grey one rising from the horizon. The blue-grey is the Earth's own shadow on the atmosphere. Turn around at sunset; most people never do.
    Cloud cut-off
    Low cloud far to the west can block the Sun before it reaches your horizon even when your sky is clear. Asterfold checks point forecasts 60 km and 180 km toward the setting Sun for exactly this, per its listing.
    Flat horizon
    What every calculator, including the one above and the app, assumes. Hills, buildings and trees are not modelled; a ridge to the west takes minutes off everything.

    Questions

    What is the difference between golden hour and blue hour?

    Which side of the horizon the Sun is on. Golden hour is the low-sun stretch around sunset and sunrise, warm and long-shadowed; blue hour is the short window after sunset (or before sunrise) when the Sun is a few degrees below and the sky turns deep blue. The calculator above gives both for your place and date; the glossary gives the angles.

    When should a winter hiker start walking down?

    At the end of civil twilight at the latest, which is when the Sun reaches 6° below the horizon; in December that can be as little as 30 minutes after sunset at mid-northern latitudes and longer further north. Have the headlamp on before that, not after. The calculator above labels it headlamp time.

    Why are December sunsets so far south?

    The Sun's setting direction swings through the year, furthest south around the 21 December solstice. A viewpoint that frames a June sunset may not frame a December one. Asterfold's Studio draws the year's range of sunset directions on the rim and has a shortcut to the year's most southerly sunset.

    Is the calculator on this page accurate?

    To within a few minutes for sea-level, flat-horizon conditions; it uses standard solar-position equations in your browser and your browser's time zone. Refraction and altitude add minutes either way. Hills to the west take more. For planning a walk down, round towards caution.
